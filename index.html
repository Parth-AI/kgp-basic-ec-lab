    How to use Lab Bench

    1. Pick the experiment and circuit from the chips at the top and the tabs above the board. The link in the address bar opens that same circuit, so you can share it.
    2. Wire the board. Click a socket, or a lead end on the tags above the board (AFG1022, CH1, CH2, MULTIMETER, DC SUPPLY), then click where the other end goes. Click the ✕ on a lead to pull it out. Check wiring tells you what is missing; Show expected connections draws dashed hints.
    3. Set the generator. Utility › System › Set to Default, then Utility › Setup › CH1 Load › High Z. Choose the waveform, type a value on the keypad and press a unit soft key, then press CH1 On/Off.
    4. Set the scope. Default, then each CH menu › Probe › 1X. Press Autoset. Measure › CH1 or CH2 opens the list: turn the multipurpose knob and push it to add Pk-Pk, Frequency and so on.
    5. Knobs: drag up or down, scroll, use the ◀ ▶ buttons, or focus a knob and press the arrow keys. Click a knob without dragging to push it.
    6. Multimeter: turn the dial, place its two leads, and use SELECT for diode test and continuity. Resistance and capacitance need the power off.
    7. Graph: press Record reading at each setting, or Auto sweep. Readings taken with different settings become separate lines. Download CSV and Save graph give you files for your record.

    Your wiring and readings are saved in this browser only.

    About Lab Bench

    A practice bench for the Basic Electronics Laboratory experiments at IIT Kharagpur: the AFG1022 function generator, TBS1102C oscilloscope, a handheld multimeter and the trainer boards, drawn from the lab manual.

    • Experiment 2 · RC, CR and RL circuits, voltage divider
    • Experiment 3 · Rectifier and power supply
    • Experiment 4 · Small-signal CE amplifier
    • Experiment 5 · Analog circuits with the 741 op-amp

    Limits. The circuits use simplified models. Component values the manual does not print are assumed, and some part positions on the op-amp board are read from photos, so check them against your own board. Diode and transistor types on the Experiment 2 board are assumed for practice.
